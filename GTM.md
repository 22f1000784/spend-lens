# Go-To-Market Strategy — SpendLens

How SpendLens acquires its first 100 users and builds a sustainable lead pipeline for Credex.

---

## Target User Profile

### Primary: Engineering Managers at Seed–Series A startups (5–30 employees)

- **Pain**: They approve AI tool purchases but have no visibility into ROI or waste
- **Behavior**: Already paying for 3–5 AI tools per team, spending $500–$3,000/mo
- **Channel**: Active on LinkedIn, Hacker News, Twitter/X, and engineering Slack communities
- **Decision power**: Can approve tool changes without executive sign-off
- **Trigger event**: They just got a finance team question about "why is our AI spend $X?"

### Secondary: Startup founders (technical CTOs/CEOs)

- **Pain**: Personally chose the tools and feel responsible for spend
- **Behavior**: Bootstrapped or pre-Series A, watching every dollar
- **Channel**: IndieHackers, Twitter/X, YC community, startup Discord servers

---

## Channel Strategy

### Channel 1: LinkedIn Organic (Primary — Week 1-2)

**Tactic**: Publish a post titled *"We audited our AI tool spend and found $4,200/year in waste. Here's the breakdown."*

**Why it works**: LinkedIn engineering content gets strong engagement when it includes specific numbers and takeaways. The post shares genuine findings from our own audit (eating our own dog food), with a CTA to "audit your own stack for free" → links to SpendLens.

**Expected reach**: 5,000–15,000 impressions with 2-5% click-through → 100-750 visits → 30-200 audits.

**Cost**: $0

### Channel 2: Hacker News / Reddit (Week 1)

**Tactic**: Submit as "Show HN: Free tool to audit your team's AI tool spend" with a technical write-up.

**Why it works**: HN rewards free tools with genuine utility and technical depth. SpendLens's deterministic audit engine + AI summary is the right balance of interesting and useful. Include a technical footnote about the architecture.

**Expected reach**: Front page gets 10,000+ visits. Even page 2 gets 500-2,000.

**Cost**: $0

### Channel 3: Twitter/X Thread (Ongoing)

**Tactic**: Weekly thread format — "AI Tool Pricing Watch: This week's changes that affect your spend" — mentioning SpendLens for the full audit.

**Why it works**: Builds authority in the AI spend space. Each thread is a distribution event. Tag tool accounts (@cursor_ai, @GitHubCopilot) for amplification.

**Expected reach**: 10-50 clicks per thread, compounding with follower growth.

**Cost**: $0

### Channel 4: Direct Outreach to Startup Communities (Week 2-3)

**Tactic**: Join 5 relevant communities (YC batch Slack, IndieHackers, specific startup Discord servers) and genuinely participate. When AI spend comes up naturally, mention SpendLens.

**Why it works**: Community-based recommendations convert at 5-10x the rate of cold traffic. The key is being genuinely helpful, not spammy.

**Expected reach**: 20-50 audits from community referrals.

**Cost**: $0

## Unfair Distribution Advantage

SpendLens has one unfair advantage that competitors don't: **every audit creates a shareable URL**.

When a user shares their audit with their CTO or finance team, that's a *warm, qualified* introduction. The shared URL shows specific savings for their team — not a generic landing page. The recipient sees "$340/mo in potential savings" and thinks "I should audit our spend too."

This creates a **viral coefficient**: each audit has a ~15% chance of generating another audit (from sharing). At 100 initial audits, this yields ~15 organic second-order audits without any marketing spend.

Additionally, high-savings audits (>$500/mo) trigger a Credex CTA that's contextually relevant — "You're spending $X/mo on AI APIs. Credex bulk credits save 20-40%." This isn't a cold pitch; it's a recommendation backed by data the user just generated.
