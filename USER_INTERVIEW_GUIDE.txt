PURPOSE
-------
You are NOT testing your app. You are doing problem discovery.
Goal: Understand if people genuinely feel the pain of overspending on AI tools,
how they currently think about it, and what would make them trust an audit tool.

This shapes your audit logic, copy, and UX decisions.
You MUST document what changed in your design because of each conversation.

IMPORTANT: These interviews MUST be real. Fabricated interviews are an instant
rejection. Three actual humans. No exceptions.


WHO TO INTERVIEW
----------------
Target people who actually pay for AI tools at work or for a side project:

  - Startup founders (solo or small team)
  - Engineering managers at small companies
  - Freelance developers with AI subscriptions
  - Friends or seniors running side projects

Where to find them:
  - LinkedIn 1st-degree connections
  - X/Twitter (cold DM)
  - College alumni / WhatsApp groups
  - Reddit: r/webdev, r/startups, r/SideProject
  - IndieHackers community
  - Discord dev servers

TIP: Start with people you already know — a friend paying for ChatGPT Plus,
a senior running a startup. Then branch out. Message 10 people so at least
3 respond in time.


SUGGESTED OUTREACH MESSAGE (LinkedIn / X DM)
--------------------------------------------
"Hey [Name], I'm building a free tool that audits AI tool spend for startups —
helps founders see if they're overpaying on Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, etc.
Would you spare 10 minutes for a quick call this week? I'm just trying to
understand the problem before I build. No sales pitch, just learning.
Happy to share the tool with you when it's live."


INTERVIEW SCRIPT (10–15 minutes)
---------------------------------

--- OPENING (2 min) ---

"Thanks for chatting! I'm building a tool to help founders and devs understand
if they're overspending on AI tools. I'd love to understand your experience —
there are no right or wrong answers, I'm just trying to learn."

[Ask if you can take notes or record — most people say yes]


--- SECTION 1: CURRENT AI TOOL USAGE (3–4 min) ---

1. "What AI tools or subscriptions are you currently paying for —
   for work or personal projects?"

2. "Roughly how much do you spend per month on them, across the team?"

3. "How many people on your team use them?"

4. "What's your main use case — coding, writing, research,
   data analysis, or a mix?"


--- SECTION 2: PAIN DISCOVERY (4–5 min) ---

5. "Have you ever looked at your AI tool bills and felt like you might be
   overpaying — or wondered if a cheaper option would work just as well?"

6. "When you last renewed or signed up for a plan, how did you decide
   which one to pick?"

7. "Do you have any sense of what the 'right' amount to spend on AI tools
   is for a team your size?"

8. "Has anyone on your team ever switched AI tools to save money?
   What happened?"


--- SECTION 3: TOOL REACTION (2–3 min) ---

9. "If you could get an instant report showing whether you're overpaying
   on AI tools — with specific alternatives — would that be useful?
   What would make you trust it?"

10. "Would you share something like that with your team or on social media
    if it showed you were overspending by $X/month?"

11. "What would make you NOT use it — what would seem sketchy
    or untrustworthy?"


--- CLOSING (1 min) ---

"Last question — is there anything about your AI tool spending that I
haven't asked about that you think is important?"

[Thank them. Offer to share the tool when it's live — most will say yes.]


HOW TO TAKE NOTES DURING THE CALL
-----------------------------------
Don't try to write everything. Focus on capturing:
  - Exact dollar amounts they mention
  - Surprising or contradictory statements
  - Moments where they push back or disagree
  - Any specific tool names or pain points they bring up unprompted
  - Their exact words (for quotes) — even rough paraphrases are fine

-----------------------------------------
Use this exact format for each interview:

  **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

  **Channel:** WhatsApp call / LinkedIn call / In-person

  ### Summary
  [2–3 sentence overview of who they are and their AI tool situation]

  ### 3+ Direct Quotes
  - "..."
  - "..."
  - "..."

  ### Most Surprising Thing They Said
  [Something you didn't expect — a contradiction, weird behavior,
  unexpected pain point]

  ### What It Changed About Your Design
  [A concrete design decision you made or reversed because of this conversation]


WHAT MAKES A GOOD VS BAD INTERVIEW ENTRY
------------------------------------------

  GOOD (scores well):                      BAD (flagged as fake):
  --------------------------------         --------------------------------
  Specific dollar amounts mentioned        Vague "they spend a lot"
  Surprising or contradictory statements   Generic "they found it useful"
  A concrete design change you made        No mention of what changed
  Quotes that sound like a real person     Polished, corporate-sounding quotes
  Confusion, pushback, or disagreement     Everything was positive/validating
